Baby fever: when to see a doctor?

Updated: September 27, 2026 · Based on: American Academy of Pediatrics
In short

For a baby under 3 months, any temperature of 38.0°C (100.4°F) or higher needs urgent medical attention, even at night. From 3 to 6 months, call your pediatrician at 38.0°C. For older children the threshold for a call is 39.0°C (102.2°F), and at 40.5°C (105°F) any child needs urgent help.

Fever thresholds by age

Child’s ageTemperatureWhat to do
Under 3 months≥ 38.0°C / 100.4°FSee a doctor urgently, even at night
3 to 6 months≥ 38.0°C / 100.4°FCall your pediatrician
Over 6 months38.5 to 39.0°C / 101.3 to 102.2°FHigh fever. A fever reducer can make your child more comfortable, dose as per the leaflet
Over 6 months≥ 39.0°C / 102.2°FCall your pediatrician
Any age≥ 40.5°C / 105°FUrgent medical help

Why is the threshold lower for the youngest babies?

In the first 3 months, a baby’s immune system is still immature, and fever may be the only sign of a serious infection. That is why guidelines recommend urgent medical assessment even when the baby otherwise looks well.

Fever lasting more than 3 days

If a child over 6 months has had a fever for more than 72 hours, call your pediatrician, even if the child seems fairly well.

Don’t just look at the thermometer

Lethargy, difficulty waking, trouble breathing, seizures or a rash that does not fade when pressed need urgent help regardless of the temperature. Full list: warning signs in babies.

Log readings with the time

The doctor will ask how long the fever has lasted, how high it was and when medicine was given. If you log readings with the time, it is easier to answer and to see whether the temperature is rising or falling.
