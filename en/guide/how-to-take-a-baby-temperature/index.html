How to take a baby’s temperature

Updated: September 29, 2026 · Based on: American Academy of Pediatrics
In short

For babies under 3 months, a rectal reading is the most accurate. The armpit is the easiest but least accurate, so double-check a doubtful reading another way. A forehead (temporal artery) thermometer works from 3 months and an ear thermometer from 6 months.

Methods by age

MethodFrom what ageAccuracy
Rectalfrom birthMost accurate, recommended under 3 months
Forehead (temporal artery)from 3 monthsGood, following the thermometer’s instructions
Earfrom 6 monthsGood, less accurate in younger babies
Armpitany ageLeast accurate, fine as a first check

How to take a rectal temperature safely

  1. Use a digital thermometer and put a little petroleum jelly on the tip.
  2. Lay your baby on their back with knees bent toward the tummy, or tummy down across your lap.
  3. Gently insert the tip about 1.5 cm (half an inch) and hold it until it beeps.
  4. Wash the thermometer with soap and water afterwards and do not use it in the mouth.

Armpit readings are lower

An armpit reading is usually lower than a rectal one. If the result is borderline, or your baby seems unwell despite a low reading, measure again with a more accurate method.

What counts as a fever?

A fever is 38.0°C (100.4°F) or higher. In a baby under 3 months, any fever needs urgent medical attention. Thresholds for older children: baby fever: when to see a doctor.
