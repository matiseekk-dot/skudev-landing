When do babies get their first teeth?

Updated: September 29, 2026 · Based on: American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry, American Academy of Pediatrics
In short

The bottom front teeth usually come first, between 6 and 10 months, followed by the top front teeth (8 to 12 months) and the side teeth. Every baby has their own pace. If there are no teeth by 18 months, ask a dentist. Teething does not cause a high fever.

Baby teeth: typical age

ToothAge (months)Notes
Bottom front6-10Usually first
Top front8-12
Top sides9-13
Bottom sides10-16
First molars13-19
Canines16-23
Second molars23-33Usually last

Signs of teething

Drooling, chewing on everything, fussiness and poorer sleep are typical. A chilled (not frozen) teether and rubbing the gums with a clean finger can help.

A fever is not teething

Teething can raise the temperature slightly, but it does not cause a fever of 38°C (100.4°F) or more. If your baby has a fever, look for another cause and contact a doctor. Thresholds by age: baby fever.

First dental visit

Book the first visit to a pediatric dentist after the first tooth appears, and no later than around the first birthday.
